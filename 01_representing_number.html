Module Section 1

The Vertical Progression: Representing Number (K–6)

Explore how the conceptual model shifts from perceptual quantity to multi-unit coordination, fractional place value, and proportional thinking.

Architecture Insight: Part A vs. Part B

In Stages 1, 2, and 3, the NSW syllabus structures content into Part A and Part B. Part A establishes standard place-value structure, foundational representations (e.g. standard grouping, number lines), and regular patterns. Part B deliberately extends this into non-standard renaming, flexible restructuring, and inverse relational applications.

Module Section 2

Foundational Concepts & Critical Pedagogical Traps

Research-informed pedagogical distinctions detailed in the NESA Teaching Advice.

Subitising & Cardinality (ES1)

Perceptual subitising is recognising small collections (up to 4) immediately without counting. Conceptual subitising relies on recognising grouped units (e.g., 6 on a dice as two 3s).

The Common Trap: Assuming a student who recites "1 to 20" understands quantity. Without the cardinality principle (knowing the final number word names the total), recitation is merely an oral song without numerical magnitude.

Composite Unit of Ten (Stage 1)

A ten is a composite unit: it must be perceived simultaneously as ten individual units AND one single collection of 10 (like 10 peas in one pod).

Track vs. Line: A numeral track has discrete boxes where numbers are counted as items. A number line is a continuous measure of length where quantity corresponds to the distance from zero.

The Dual Roles of Zero (Stage 2)

Zero operates in two fundamentally distinct ways in place value:

  • Lexical Zeros: Zeros at the end of numbers that name the quantity (e.g., 300 names "three hundred").
  • Syntactic Zeros: Internal zeros acting as null place holders (e.g., the zeros in 1002).
Pedagogical Insight: Syntactic zeros present much higher cognitive load. Without explicit parsing, students frequently transcode 1002 as "one hundred and two" or write 10010 for "one hundred and ten".

Symmetry Around the 'Ones' (Stage 2/3)

A deeply entrenched misunderstanding is that place value is symmetrical around the decimal point. It is not.

Tens ← [ ONES ] → Tenths
Hundreds ← [ ONES ] → Hundredths

The ones column is the true centre of symmetry. Multiplying by 10 builds units to the left; dividing by 10 partitions units to the right. The decimal point merely marks where the ones end.

Module Section 3

Interactive Simulation: The NESA Number Slide

Experience the key pedagogical shift in Stage 2/3: "The decimal point does NOT move; the digits shift across place-value columns."

Why this matters (NESA Stage 3 Teaching Advice)

"A common misunderstanding is the belief that the decimal point moves when a number is multiplied by a power of ten. The number slide, with its fixed decimal marker, is an effective way of addressing this. Multiplying by 10 increases the value of every digit by a factor of 10, causing each digit to shift one place to the left."

Module Section 4

Student Diagnostics: Analysing Authentic Student Work

Review real student dialogue and error patterns adapted directly from NESA research trials.

Diagnostic Case 1: Stage 1 (Ethan, Year 2) Topic: Digit-Correspondence Task (Ross, 2002)

Teacher: [Presents 16 tiles] "Ethan, how many tiles are on the table?"

Ethan: [Counts accurately by ones] "Sixteen." [Writes 16]

Teacher: [Points to the 6 in 16] "Can you show me with the tiles what this 6 represents?"

Ethan: [Pushes 6 tiles to the side] "Three and three. Six."

Teacher: [Points to the 1 in 16] "And what does this 1 represent for the tiles?"

Ethan: [Picks up 1 single tile and places it next to the 1] "It stands for one unit."

Teacher: "So altogether we have 6 tiles and 1 tile... that's 7 tiles. What happens to the other 9 tiles?"

Ethan: "Oh, those are just the leftovers!"

Module Section 5

Task Makeover: Embedding Working Mathematically

Transform procedural "worksheet drills" into rich mathematical investigations integrating Reasoning and Communicating (MAO-WM-01).

Before: Closed Procedural Task

Worksheet Question 4:

Underline the digit in the hundreds place:

5, 4 2 9

State its value: ___________

Limitations:

  • Tests only rote column recognition.
  • Encourages single-digit focus without multi-unit flexibility.
  • Zero opportunities for mathematical justification or reasoning.

After: Working Mathematically Makeover

Inquiry Prompt (MA2-RN-01 & MAO-WM-01):

"Maya claims the number 5429 has 54 hundreds.
Leo says it only has 4 hundreds.
Who is mathematically correct? Prove your answer using base-10 materials, a number expander, or a diagram."

Follow-up Extension (Wipe-Out Challenge):
"Enter 5429 into a calculator. In a single subtraction, make the display read 5029. What exact number did you subtract, and what place value does that represent?"

Working Mathematically Elements:

  • Reasoning: Explores standard vs non-standard partitioning (54 hundreds + 29 ones).
  • Communicating: Students must justify why both students are correct from different perspectives.
  • Fluency: Builds foundational flexibility needed for multi-digit mental algorithms.

Question 3 (Stage 2)
