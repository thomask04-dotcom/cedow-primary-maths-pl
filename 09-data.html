Collecting & Organising

From physical objects as data (ES1), to one-to-one picture graphs (S1), column graphs and dot plots with many-to-one scales (S2), to line graphs and timelines (S3).

Moving from reading the data explicitly ("Which has the most?"), to inferring meaning ("What does this tell us?"), to generalising beyond ("Would another class be the same?").

Critical Reading

Section 2: Key Conceptual Shifts & Pedagogical Traps

Research-informed deep dives into how student thinking must evolve, and common instructional traps identified by NESA Teaching Advice.

Early Stage 1 → Stage 1

Shift: From physical objects arranged into displays, to symbols that abstractly represent a data value. Gathering moves to structured tallying.

Pedagogical Trap Students judging quantity by length/area rather than count. For example, believing that larger banana symbols mean bananas are more popular, even if there are fewer of them. Always enforce: baseline, equal spacing, same-sized symbols.

Stage 1 → Stage 2

Shift: From picture graphs to column graphs/dot plots with labelled axes, equal intervals, and the introduction of many-to-one scales.

Pedagogical Trap Reading a many-to-one scale as one-to-one (e.g., reading a column reaching the third gridline as 3, when each interval represents 2). Or joining the columns of a column graph (suggesting continuous rather than discrete categorical data).

Stage 2 → Stage 3

Shift: From discrete data to continuous data over time (line graphs and timelines), choosing appropriate displays, and identifying bias.

Pedagogical Trap Using line graphs for categorical data. Points between categories (e.g., between "Apples" and "Bananas") have no meaning. Line graphs should only be used where points between plotted values have meaning (e.g., temperature over time).

Key Pedagogical Takeaways (K–6)

  • ES1: Sort physical objects and compare counts. Acknowledge personal beliefs bias predictions.
  • Stage 1: Transition to abstract symbols (one-to-one). Enforce baseline, equal spacing, and uniform sizing in picture graphs.
  • Stage 2: Introduce many-to-one scales. Focus on discrete data (column graphs, dot plots). Teach "Here, Hidden, Beyond" inference.
  • Stage 3: Match the display to the data. Line graphs for continuous change, column graphs for discrete. Recognise bias, selection flaws, and misleading axes.
